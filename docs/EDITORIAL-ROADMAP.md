## What makes each one worth publishing (working angles)

1. **Cables**: a safety-first plan, built around where power lives and the daisy-chain warning, rather than a product list. *Done.*
2. **Arm vs stand**: compatibility checks (VESA, minimum weight, desk edge) that most comparisons skip. *Done.*
3. **Keyboard sizes**: a real width comparison using published manufacturer dimensions. *Done.*
4. **Compact keyboard**: organized by which keys you rely on, not by brand. Needs verified product facts and real Amazon links.
5. **Risers**: when a riser beats an arm, with a depth and height planning worksheet.
6. **Dual monitors**: a decision tree covering matching heights, a primary vs symmetric layout, and total arm load.
7. **Standing desk accessories**: only accessories that solve sit-stand problems, like cable slack, mat use and monitor height.
8. **Desk lighting**: glare and placement explained before any product picks. Needs reliable lighting sources.
9. **Budget clean desk**: a priority order for spending, starting with the free steps.
10. **Small rooms**: layout patterns with original illustrations. A good fit for Pinterest.
11. **Clutter**: a daily, weekly and monthly item audit method.
12. **Small home office accessories**: a problem-to-accessory matrix.
13. **Programmers**: layout, keyboard and screen-space advice. Avoid gadget filler.
14. **Keyboard and mouse combos**: by work style, such as spreadsheet, coding, design or gaming.
15. **Headset vs speakers**: shared spaces, calls, microphone pickup and desk space.
16. **Streaming setup**: audio-first priority order, plus mounting and power planning.
17. **Gaming desk upgrades**: mouse space, cable routing and screen distance.
18. **Problem-solving accessories**: one problem per section, with the free fix listed first.
19. **Students**: dorm constraints, portability and budget tiers. Describe tiers without prices.
20. **WFH gifts**: practical items with "who it's for" framing. No invented deals.

## Cadence suggestion

Aim to publish one or two thoroughly researched guides a week rather than many thin ones. Revisit published guides quarterly to re-verify product facts and sources.
